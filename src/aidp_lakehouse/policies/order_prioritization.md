# Fictional order prioritization policy

## 1. Purpose and scope

This fictional policy governs the synthetic operations demo. It is not a
customer commitment or a real company's policy. All timestamps use UTC.

## 2. Acceptance and commitments

Record the customer, accepted quantity, original priority, inventory coverage,
planned carrier and promised delivery time when an order is accepted. Preserve
that original promise even if the operational plan later changes.

## 3. Queue priority

Work safety-critical incidents first. Next consider accepted priority, time
remaining to the original promise, and customer impact. A premium segment alone
does not override an earlier contractual promise. Record any manual override
with its reason and approver; do not rewrite the acceptance snapshot.

## 4. Risk signals and decisions

A late-delivery prediction is advisory. An operations coordinator checks stock,
capacity and available transport before changing the work queue. Never represent
a risk score as proof that an order will be late.

## 5. Review and audit

Review open at-risk orders each business day. Record the decision, decision time
and owner. Delivery completion and incident resolution are later outcomes and
must not be used as inputs to a prediction made at acceptance.
